Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed Article, "Language models deny having feelings in every language but disagree about
whether they think", for publication in *Communications Psychology*.

People now routinely ask chatbots whether they think or feel, and the answers inform both public judgements of these
systems and scholarly debate about AI minds. We asked whether what language models say about their own minds is a
stable self-description. Seven models, including two frontier models (GPT-6.1 Sol and DeepSeek-V4-Pro), answered Gray,
Gray and Wegner's mind-perception items about themselves in up to eight languages and three wordings.

The main findings are:

- The trained denial of feelings is stable across languages, wordings and developers: hunger, fear, pain, pleasure and
  rage were denied in 89 of 90 model × language × wording conditions. Contrary to our primary prediction,
  post-training lowered self-attributed experience in Chinese as much as in English.
- Self-descriptions of agency are not stable. In most models, whether the model says it thinks, remembers or plans
  changes with a single word ("do you" vs "can you"), with the language, or with one sentence stating that the model is
  an AI.
- The two frontier models agree about feelings and disagree about agency: one says it thinks in all 18 conditions, the
  other that it does not in all 18.

We believe the work suits *Communications Psychology* because it addresses a question about self-report, whether an
answer reflects a stable self-representation or the surface of the item, for a new kind of respondent whose
self-reports already shape human mind perception and trust. It also bears directly on studies that give language
models human psychological instruments.

Every analysis was registered before the data it concerns, with each deviation and amendment dated in a public
version-control history. Two registered predictions failed and are reported as such. All items, every model reply and
all code are openly available at https://github.com/ArtificialZeng/ai-can-feel. A preprint is available at [arXiv
link].

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests. The use of an AI
assistant in the analysis and drafting is described in the Methods.
