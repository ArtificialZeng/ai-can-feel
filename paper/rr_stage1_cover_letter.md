Dear Editors,

Please consider our Stage 1 Registered Report, "What language models say about their own minds, and what people make
of it", for *Nature Human Behaviour*.

**The question.** People now ask chatbots whether they think or feel, and the answers circulate as evidence about AI
minds. We ask two things. Are these self-descriptions stable properties of the systems, or of the questions? And do
they change what people believe about AI?

**Why we expect an answer worth having.** Five pre-registered pilot studies, with every plan and amendment dated in a
public version-control history, gave a clear and unexpected pattern. Across the six of seven language models with valid data in every language
tested, hunger, fear, pain, pleasure and rage were denied in 89 of 90 model × language × wording conditions. Whether
the models said they think, remember or plan changed with a single word, the language, or a reminder that they are AI. Two frontier models from different developers agreed about feelings and gave
opposite answers about thinking. If this holds across deployed models, and if people take a chatbot's word about its
own mind, then each developer's choice of what its model says about thinking becomes a lever on public belief about
AI minds, in every language the model is used in.

**What the Registered Report will do.**
- *Study 6:* 1,600 adults in five languages will answer the same questions as the models, about themselves and about
  AI, after reading a chatbot's real answer saying it does or does not think, or a neutral answer. This tests the human
  consequence (H1) and whether the models' wording effects belong to the questions (H2).
- *Study 7:* 30 widely used models, selected by a fixed rule from a public usage ranking, will test whether the two
  pilot contrasts generalize (H3, H4).
- Every confirmatory test has power of at least 0.95 at a stated smallest effect of interest. For H2 this assumes
  that adults answer "yes" to each agency question about themselves at least 75% of the time. For the model study,
  power was simulated from the pilot.

**Why a Registered Report.** The pilot results are striking enough that a reader should be able to trust that the
confirmatory tests were fixed before the data. The project has been run this way from the start. Two of the pilot
predictions failed and are reported as such.
